# Compute benchmarks — 2026-10-01, Snapdragon X Elite (X1E80100), Phase 323 (streamed GroupBy aggregates)

Phase 323 replaced the `GroupBy` step's per-group member lists, boxed cell lists, per-group
`Column` and row-by-row `Frame.ofRows` with one pass over the rows into per-slot accumulators
(`DataFrame.GroupAgg`), written straight into typed output vectors, and probed a single string or
int key by its carrier value rather than by a boxed cell. The incremental seam's dirty-group
recompute reads the same accumulators. `Column.aggregate` stays the one aggregate semantics: every
case a stream does not reproduce outright it defers to `Column.aggregate` over the group's members,
and a differential law (`streamedAggregateLaws`) holds every streamed answer equal to it.

## Instrument

.NET 10 (SDK 10.0.401), Release, a console probe referencing the benchmark project's Release build
(`Corpus.fs` directly), compiled once against the pre-phase tree (`43d1893`) and once against this
phase's tree. Three warm-up calls; calls per sample doubled until a sample spans 50 ms; seven samples
with a forced GC before each; best and median per call. Allocation is
`GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread` across a sample divided by its calls, so it is the calling
thread's bytes and is the same on every run. The two arms were run **paired and interleaved** (new,
pre-phase, new, pre-phase, over three rounds), and each figure below is the arm's best across them.

The machine was **not quiet**: other sessions held it busy throughout, and a pre-phase arm read
anywhere from 20 ms to 46 ms for the same 100,000-row step. The allocation figures do not move with
load; the times do, and are why only same-run ratios are claimed. The step measured is
`DataFrame.evalPrepared` of `[GroupBy(region; total = Sum amount, n = Count amount)]` over a prepared
`region, amount` table ("prepared"), and `DataFrame.evalPipeline` of the same over the `Table`
("Table in", which adds the boundary's unpack of every input cell).

## The in-place refresh (the operator ruling of 2026-10-01)

The ruling: extend the phase until the four group-by nodes hold the standing 1.6x, with refresh equal
to full byte for byte. The split above says where the tick's time is, so the refresh was made to pay
for the DELTA:

* `Delta.diff` records, beside a delta it produced, when every row of the new table sits where it
  sat in the old one (same count, same key at every index) and which rows' content moved.
* `Incremental.refresh`, given such a delta against the very table its state was evaluated over,
  shares the prior row tokens, builds the prior-slot and stability arrays without a lookup per row,
  does not walk an unedited column's list to count it, starts each `Filter` / `Derive` step from the
  prior evaluation's cells and evaluates only the changed rows (reading those rows cell by cell
  rather than unpacking the column), and reuses the prior planned pipeline.
* The maintained `GroupBy` (`groupStepInPlace`) takes the prior partition, group order, member lists
  and group rows when every changed row stays in its group and is not a group's first row, and
  recomputes only the changed rows' groups. Anything else declines to the general walk.
* 323.t3, now measurable: on `byRegion` at 100,000 rows the one dirty group's recompute was about 4
  to 6 ms of an 8 to 10 ms refresh (an otherwise identical `GroupBy` with no aggregates refreshed in
  3.4 to 5.0 ms). `Count` and an int `Sum` are now maintained by subtracting each changed member's
  prior contribution and adding its new one; an int `Sum` and `Count` over `qty` then refreshed
  within about 1.7 to 1.9 ms of the no-aggregate pipeline, against 4.5 to 5.5 ms before. A float
  `Sum` is still rescanned over its members, so its bits stay the left fold.

## The 1,000-row tick on CI, and the fixed cost behind it (fix forward, 2026-10-02)

After the landing, CI on main (windows-latest, quiet: steady calibration, nothing discarded) was red
on one case: the Scaling tick `filter > groupBy` at 1,000 rows read 2.55 / 1.89 / 1.87x (diff +
refresh 0.29 / 0.21 / 0.20 ms against a full evaluation of 0.11 ms). The streamed evaluator had made
the small full evaluation so cheap that the tick's per-call cost dominated. A compiled probe of that
exact case (same table, edit and pipeline) on this machine:

| 1,000 rows, us per call (best of nine) | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| full evaluation | 103.8-152.0 | 103.8-157.9 |
| `Delta.diff` | 121.0-323.9 | 26.8-53.6 |
| `Delta.diff` allocation | 71,964 B | 3,702 B |
| refresh (same delta) | 40.5-51.0 | 33.1-62.0 |
| tick (fresh table, diff, refresh) | 189.8-305.0 | 74.6-125.5 |
| tick / full | 1.42-2.01 | 0.68-0.90 |

The diff was the fixed cost: on every tick it keyed the fresh table row by row (the key column
unpacked, an id and an option per row, a typed equality per row) and unpacked both copies of the
edited column into arrays, although the clock's edit, like a consumer's in-place edit, leaves the
key column's cell list the very object it was. Now a reference witness DECLARES the columns its key
reads (`RowIdentity.byColumn` / `byColumns`), and a diff whose key columns are the same lists in
both tables, over one row count, takes every row as in place with the prior table's keys and ids,
without keying one; and every changed column is compared by walking the two lists in step instead of
unpacking them. A law in the suite holds the shared-list diff equal to the diff of the same pair with
the key lists copied (every row keyed), for one key column and for two, refusals included. The
in-place `GroupBy` also builds its member lists only when an aggregate is rescanned.

The Release clock leg after it (two runs, exit 0, every tick case green on its first counted
attempt): the Scaling `filter > groupBy` at 1,000 rows read 0.75 and 0.66 (0.11 ms against 0.16 ms
in a counted window whose calibration readings stayed within 1.25x); every corpus group-by node read
0.35 to 0.56 at 1,000 rows and 0.35 to 0.50 at 100,000; the worst node was `window CumulSum` at
1.35 (Phase 333's).

## The share figures behind 265.t4

265.t4 (a compute-side aggregate fold) was deferred while `Column.aggregate` was a small share of the
step: 8.4 percent, then 13.5 at its widest. The 2026-09-30 review measured the aggregates at about 68
percent of `byRegion`'s 100,000-row step once the grouping itself had been made allocation-free. This
phase closes 265.t4 with the differential law in place of the argument that deferred it.
