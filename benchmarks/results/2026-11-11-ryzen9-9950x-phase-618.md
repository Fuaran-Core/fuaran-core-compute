# Compute benchmarks — 2026-11-11, AMD Ryzen 9 9950X, Phase 618 (the pinned distributions)

Phase 618 pins the cumulative distribution function and the quantile of the standard normal, Student's t, the
chi-square and Fisher's F distributions (`Fuaran.Compute.Distributions`, `DECISIONS.md` D79). This file records the
measurement the decision rests on: each function's error against a fifty-digit reference by range, its cost per
value on .NET and under node, the candidates each choice was made between, and the chi-square quantile's iteration
counts.

## How it was taken

- Machine: AMD Ryzen 9 9950X (16 cores), Windows 11; .NET SDK 10.0.203 (Release, x64), node v22.23.4
  (Fable 5.0.0, the repository's tool manifest).
- Harness: the node leg's timing program (`benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node`), family
  `distributions` (`../DistributionsBench.fs`), the same program on both hosts:
  - .NET: `dotnet run -c Release --project benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node -- 5 distributions > dotnet.txt`
  - node: `pwsh benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node/run-node.ps1 -FableFrom . -Runs 5 -Only distributions > node.txt`
  - the check: the lines between `BEGIN distributions-samples` and `END distributions-samples`, through
    `python tests/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Tests/DistributionsReference/distributions_reference.py check`
- The sample is a seeded draw at 618 through the pinned Park–Miller generator and the pinned `exp`, `ln` and
  `pow`, so it is the same 11,640 inputs on every host (and 13,120 more lines, the candidates' answers over the
  same inputs). Per function:
  - `Normal.cdf`: 600 uniform over `(−38, 9)`. `Normal.quantile`: 600 probabilities.
  - A probability, wherever one is drawn: uniform over `(0, 1)` six draws in ten, within twelve decades of zero two
    in ten, within twelve decades of one two in ten.
  - `StudentT.cdf`: `ν` log-uniform over `[0.05, 100)` (1,200) and over `[100, 1e6)` (800); `x` uniform over
    `(−9, 9)` seven draws in ten, of either sign and a magnitude log-uniform over `[1e-8, 1e8]` otherwise.
    `StudentT.quantile`: the same two ranges of `ν`, each with a probability.
  - `ChiSquare.cdf`: `k` log-uniform over `[1e-3, 2)` (600), `[2, 100)` (600), `[100, 2e3)` (400) and
    `[2e3, 2e5)` (60); `x` within seven standard deviations of `k` seven draws in ten, across four and a half
    decades round it otherwise. `ChiSquare.quantile`: the same ranges of `k`, each with a probability.
  - `FisherF.cdf`: `(d1, d2)` log-uniform over `[0.1, 1e3)²` (500), `[0.1, 10) × [100, 4e4)` (500),
    `[100, 4e4) × [0.1, 10)` (500) and `[1e3, 4e4)²` (60); `x` log-uniform over `[1e-3, 1e3]` six draws in ten and
    over `[1e-12, 1e12]` otherwise. `FisherF.quantile`: the same ranges, each with a probability.
- Each timing: a warm-up of at least half a second, then the median of the measured samples, each enough passes to
  span fifty milliseconds (Phase 593's timer, unchanged). The machine was not quiet: the captures were taken three
  times on each host and a function's reading moved by up to half between them. The cost table states the lowest
  of the three for each, and all three are in the last section.
- The reference is mpmath 1.4.1 at fifty digits, rounded once to the nearest double; the distance is in units in
  the last place of that double. A quantile's reference is the root of the distribution function nearest the
  package's answer (four Newton steps at fifty digits). An input whose true value is below `1e-290` in magnitude,
  whose answer is zero or infinite, or that mpmath cannot take, is not scored (the last column). The script's
  header says how each function is spelled.
- The two hosts' sample lines are compared byte for byte: that is the byte-identity claim. They were identical,
  all 24,760, in each of the three pairs of captures.

## Error against the reference, in units in the last place

The parameter ranges are scored apart because the error is the special functions' own and grows with a
parameter (`DECISIONS.md` D78 item 10 measured them to an incomplete beta parameter of twenty thousand, which is
`ν`, `d1` or `d2` of forty thousand).

| function | range | scored | max | 99th percentile | mean | not scored |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Normal.cdf | | 579 | 1.9 | 1.6 | 0.46 | 21 |
| Normal.quantile | | 600 | 3.5 | 2.9 | 0.86 | 0 |
| StudentT.cdf | `ν < 100` | 1195 | 560 | 81 | 5.3 | 5 |
| | `100 ≤ ν < 4e4` | 464 | 6,095 | 1,969 | 104 | 21 |
| | `4e4 ≤ ν < 1e6` | 289 | 137,400 | 66,190 | 3,580 | 26 |
| StudentT.quantile | `ν < 100` | 1200 | 314 | 92 | 8.0 | 0 |
| | `100 ≤ ν < 4e4` | 515 | 2,509 | 634 | 27 | 0 |
| | `4e4 ≤ ν < 1e6` | 284 | 24,590 | 18,700 | 796 | 1 |
| ChiSquare.cdf | `k < 2e3` | 1571 | 656 | 208 | 9.9 | 29 |
| | `2e3 ≤ k < 2e5` | 44 | 63 | 63 | 9.9 | 16 |
| ChiSquare.quantile | `k < 2e3` | 1509 | 2,380 | 348 | 17 | 91 |
| | `2e3 ≤ k < 2e5` | 60 | 0.60 | 0.60 | 0.26 | 0 |
| FisherF.cdf | `d1, d2 < 1e3` | 831 | 1,198 | 364 | 20 | 56 |
| | a parameter in `[1e3, 4e4)` | 610 | 15,800 | 2,817 | 127 | 63 |
| FisherF.quantile | `d1, d2 < 1e3` | 884 | 320 | 163 | 16 | 0 |
| | a parameter in `[1e3, 4e4)` | 676 | 3,143 | 1,004 | 63 | 0 |

Where each maximum comes from:

- **The incomplete beta function at one large and one small parameter.** `I_x(a, ½)` with `x` within a few
  `1 / a` of one is the t's tail at a `t²` of three to ten, and `Special.regularizedBeta` is out there by an
  amount that grows with `a`: measured directly against mpmath at a `t²` of `3.84`, its relative
  error in units of `2⁻⁵²` is 260 at `a = 500`, 700 at `5,000`, 6,400 at `20,000`, 19,000 at `50,000` and
  200,000 at `500,000`. That is the whole of the t's
  figures from `ν = 100` up (its maxima are at `|t|` between `1.85` and `2.9`), and of the F's with one large
  degree of freedom. It is the special function's error, outside the range D78 measured, and nothing in this
  package can remove it: the reading of a tail at a `ν` in the millions is good to eleven digits, at forty
  thousand to thirteen, at a thousand to fourteen.
- **A large exponent.** A tail far out is `exp` of an exponent of magnitude `E` and carries about `2E` units
  (D78): `StudentT.cdf`'s 560 is at `ν = 90`, `x = −4,878`, a tail of `5e-245`, and `ChiSquare.cdf`'s 656 is at
  `k = 225`, `x = 0.34`, a probability of `1e-270`.
- **Conditioning.** A quantile moves by the function's error divided by the density times the quantile. Where
  the distribution's mass is at zero that is `2 / k` or `2 / d`: `ChiSquare.quantile`'s 2,380 is at `k = 0.0015`
  (an answer of `6e-259`), `StudentT.quantile`'s 314 at `ν = 0.057`.
- **Not scored.** `ChiSquare.quantile`'s 91: 87 inputs whose quantile is below the smallest double, answered `0`,
  and four below `1e-290`. The rest are tails below `1e-290`, and distribution functions that answered exactly
  one, where the reference rounds to one too.

## The candidates

Each candidate is written in `DistributionsBench.fs` over the public special functions and scored over the same
inputs. Largest error, by the same ranges:

| function | candidate | first range | second | third |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| StudentT.cdf | shipped | 560 | 6,095 | 137,400 |
| | `direct`: `½ I_z(ν/2, ½)` at `z = ν / (ν + x²)` everywhere | 3.9e8 | 2.3e9 | 1.9e10 |
| | `split`: `½ ∓ ½ I_w(½, ν/2)` where `2x² < ν`, `direct` beyond | 1.5e8 | 8.7e15 | 7.5e15 |
| | `near-one`: shipped without its centre arm | 560 | 6,095 | 137,400 |
| StudentT.quantile | shipped | 314 | 2,509 | 24,590 |
| | `direct`: the one root of `I_z(ν/2, ½) = 2p` | 2.9e5 | 1.8e8 | 2.2e9 |
| | `two-inverses`: that root to one half, the root of `I_w(½, ν/2) = 1 − 2p` beyond | 2.6e7 | 3.1e9 | 1.3e9 |
| FisherF.cdf | shipped | 1,198 | 15,800 | |
| | `direct`: `I_w(d1/2, d2/2)` at `w = d1 x / (d1 x + d2)` everywhere | 4.0e11 | 1.8e15 | |
| FisherF.quantile | shipped | 320 | 3,143 | |
| | `direct`: the one root of `I_w(d1/2, d2/2) = p` | 1.1e15 | 7.8e13 | |

What each shows:

- **`direct` loses the argument.** `ν / (ν + x²)` near one keeps `x` only to `ν / x²` units: at `x = 5e-8` the
  distribution function is one half exactly, which is 4e8 units from the truth, and `d1 x / (d1 x + d2)` rounds to
  one while a tenth of the F's mass is still beyond it. The shipped functions form whichever of the argument and
  its complement is small, and where the function wants the other, read it through the density (`betaNearOne`).
- **`split` loses the tail.** One half minus a value near one half has the accuracy of one half: a tail of
  `1e-16` is all rounding. This is the form that needs the incomplete beta function's complement, which `Special`
  does not publish.
- **`near-one` is the shipped function's accuracy at a higher cost.** In the centre the incomplete beta function
  already reads its fraction at the small argument, so the correction buys nothing there; the centre arm saves
  an exponential, two logarithms and a `betaLn` (204 against 228 ns on .NET, 398 against 469 under node).
- **A quantile from one inverse loses the root.** `Special.inverseRegularizedBeta` answers `x`, and where the
  root is near one the quantity wanted is `1 − x`: `direct`'s errors are at `p` near one half for the t and at a
  small `d2` for the F. `two-inverses` repairs the centre and then fails in the tail of a large `ν`, where
  `1 − 2p` has rounded away the tail (`ν = 3,008`, `p = 1e-12`: 3e9 units). The shipped t takes ONE Newton step on
  its own distribution function there; the shipped F reads the mirrored inverse only where its argument is exact
  or the density says its rounding costs less.

## The chi-square quantile's iteration

`Root.bracketedNewton` under `RelativeChange (2⁻⁴⁰, 2⁻¹⁰²²)` and a cap of one hundred, over the 1,660 sampled
`(k, p)`; 87 have a quantile below the smallest double and run no solve.

| start | solved | refused at the cap | mean iterations | most |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the leading term at zero where `k/2 < −0.62 ln p`, else Wilson and Hilferty (shipped) | 1,573 | 0 | 3.52 | 27 |
| Wilson and Hilferty alone | 1,426 | 147 | 8.38 | 98 |
| half the bracket | 1,426 | 147 | 12.50 | 98 |

The 147 are small `k` with a small `p`, where Wilson and Hilferty's cube is negative or far above the root and
bisection from the bracket's middle cannot reach a root tens of decades below it in a hundred halvings. The
shipped start's longest run (27) is an upper tail of `1e-10` at `k = 0.005`, where Newton on an exponential tail
gains about one unit of `x` an iteration.
