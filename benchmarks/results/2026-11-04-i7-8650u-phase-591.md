# Compute benchmarks — 2026-11-04, Intel Core i7-8650U, Phase 591 (the streaming Givens QR)

Phase 591 lands least squares as a streaming Givens QR (`QRAccumulator`, `DECISIONS.md` D51) whose every
operation runs in D49's pinned order. This file records what that order costs at the plan's target sizes:
n ∈ {10⁵, 10⁶} rows by p ∈ {4, 16, 64} columns, the rotation's portable member against its native (SIMD) one on
.NET, and the one member under node.

## How it was taken

- Machine: Intel Core i7-8650U (4 cores, AVX2), Windows 11; .NET 10 (Release,
  x64 native process), node v22.20.0 (Fable 5.0.0, the repository's tool manifest).
- Harness: the node leg's timing program (`benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node`), family `qr`
  (`../QrBench.fs`), the same program on both hosts:
  - .NET: `dotnet run -c Release --project benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node -- 3 qr`
  - node: `pwsh benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node/run-node.ps1 -FableFrom . -Runs 3 -Only qr`
- The rows are GENERATED as they are folded (a fixed integer pattern, dense, unit weights), never stored, so
  the figure is the kernel's and not a matrix's allocation — which is how the accumulator is meant to be fed.
- Each case: a warm-up fold of 10⁴ rows, then the median of 3 folds at 10⁵ rows, one fold at 10⁶.
- Before either arm is timed the harness folds 2,000 rows through each and requires the two states equal to
  the bit (the law `LinearAlgebraLaws` holds too).
- GFLOP/s counts `3p(p + 1) + 10p` operations a row: `p(p + 1)/2` rotated pairs at four products and two sums,
  and per column the radius, cosine and sine.

The 10⁵-row figures at p = 4 are dominated by per-fold overhead and JIT tiering (the per-row cost halves at
10⁶). The native rotation pays at p = 64, where a row of `R` holds up to 64 pairs: **1.3 to 1.4 times** the
portable member. At p = 4 and 16 a row of `R` is 1 to 16 pairs, mostly shorter than the four-lane AVX2 vector
plus its tail, and the two arms are within noise of each other.

The first node run, with the rotation indexing its arrays through Fable's shared bounds-checked helper, took
**140.2 s** at 10⁶ × 64 (0.09 GFLOP/s) and 11.7 s at 10⁶ × 16. The rotation loop now indexes through
unchecked accessors under JavaScript (`Kernels.get` / `set`, the dataframe package's Phase 326 pattern), which
cut node's cost **9.5 times** at p = 64 and **4.2 times** at p = 16 with no change to any bit — the vectors and
laws are byte-identical under node before and after. node is now **2.9 times** .NET's native member at p = 64.

## The gap to a vendor BLAS (plan §6 risk) — an estimate, not a measurement

No vendor BLAS (MKL, OpenBLAS) is installed on this machine, so the comparison below is arithmetic over
published throughput, labelled as such. A Householder QR of a 10⁶ × 64 design (LAPACK `dgeqrf`) does about
`2np² − 2p³/3` ≈ 8.2 × 10⁹ operations against the streaming Givens fold's ≈ 13.1 × 10⁹; a blocked, FMA-using
`dgeqrf` on one AVX2 core of this class sustains on the order of 10 to 20 GFLOP/s at p = 64 (the panel is
narrow, so much of it runs at level-2 speed), i.e. roughly **0.4 to 0.8 s** against the native member's
**5.0 s**: a gap of **about 6 to 12 times on one core**, and several times more for a multithreaded vendor
build. The gap is the price of the contract, and it is the expected one: the pinned order forbids FMA (D49
item 2), blocking over the reduction dimension and tall-skinny splitting (D49 item 12), each of which a vendor
kernel uses. What the contract buys is that the 5.0 s produce the same bits on every host. The streaming state
is O(p²), so the cost is linear in n with no memory pressure: a fit of 10⁶ rows by 64 columns never holds more
than 64 × 65 floats.

## Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Native against portable, bit for bit | **Equal** at every width (asserted before timing, and by `LinearAlgebraLaws`) |
| What the native rotation buys | **1.3 to 1.4 times at p = 64**; nothing measurable at p ≤ 16 |
| The fold at the largest target (10⁶ × 64) | **5.0 s** on .NET (native), **14.7 s** under node |
| Against a vendor BLAS | an estimated **6 to 12 times** slower than single-threaded `dgeqrf`, unmeasured here |
