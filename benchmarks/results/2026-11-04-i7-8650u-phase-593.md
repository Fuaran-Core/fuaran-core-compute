# Compute benchmarks — 2026-11-04, Intel Core i7-8650U, Phase 593 (the pinned elementary functions)

Phase 593 pins `exp`, `ln`, `log10` and the float-exponent `pow` (`Fuaran.Compute.LinearAlgebra.Elementary`,
`DECISIONS.md` D52) so that every host computes the same bits. This file records the measurement the decision
rests on: the faithful-pinned build the package ships against a correctly rounded prototype, in cost per value
on .NET and under node, in error against an eighty-digit reference, and in lines per twin.

## How it was taken

- Machine: Intel Core i7-8650U (4 cores, AVX2), Windows 11; .NET 10 (Release, x64 native process),
  node v22.20.0 (Fable 5.0.0, the repository's tool manifest).
- Harness: the node leg's timing program (`benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node`), family
  `elementary` (`../ElementaryBench.fs`), the same program on both hosts:
  - .NET: `dotnet run -c Release --project benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node -- 5 elementary > dotnet.txt`
  - node: `pwsh benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node/run-node.ps1 -FableFrom . -Runs 5 -Only elementary > node.txt`
  - the check: `python benchmarks/results/2026-11-04-i7-8650u-phase-593/reference-check.py dotnet.txt node.txt`
- The sample is a seeded draw at 593 through the pinned Park–Miller generator (the same values on every host):
  `exp` over 3,320 inputs (2,000 uniform across `[−745.13, 709.78]`, 400 round zero, the powers of two down to
  `2⁻⁶⁰` and their negatives, 200 round the half-ln2 reduction boundary, 200 at each edge, 200 round the
  smallest normal result); `ln` and `log10` over 3,107 (2,000 log-uniform across the positive doubles, 600 within
  `10⁻³` of one, `1 ± 2⁻ᵏ` to `k = 52`, 200 round `√2`, 200 subnormals, the largest double, the smallest subnormal
  and the smallest normal); `pow` over 3,847 pairs (3,000 with bases across two hundred binary decades and
  exponents in `±30`, 600 with bases within `10⁻⁶` of one and exponents to `±10⁸`, 300 with bases a few ulps from
  one and exponents to `±10¹⁵`, 300 near the overflow edge, 300 with bases across the whole range under exponents
  in `[0, 1)`, 200 negative bases under integer exponents — every pair whose answer is finite and non-zero).
- Each case: a warm-up of at least half a second (the .NET runtime promotes a method to its optimised tier only
  after a delay, and a pass over three thousand values takes under a millisecond — the first run measured
  unoptimised code), then the median of 5 samples, each sample enough passes to span fifty milliseconds (the
  clock under node is a millisecond). The checksum is a float sum: a 64-bit integer under node is a BigInt, and
  its conversion cost more than the function being timed in the second run.
- The reference is Python's `decimal` at eighty digits — `exp`, `ln`, `log10`, and `pow` as `exp(y · ln x)` —
  rounded once to the nearest double by `float()`; the distance is the difference of ordered bit patterns.
- The two captures are compared line for line: that is the byte-identity claim.

## The candidates

- **Faithful-pinned** — the package's `Elementary`: fdlibm's `exp`, `ln` and `log10` with their published
  coefficients; `pow` as `exp(y · ln x)` with the logarithm and the product in double-double arithmetic; the
  powers of two from a table built by exact doubling; `+ − × ÷` and comparisons only.
- **Correctly rounded** — a Ziv two-phase prototype in the harness: the same reductions in double-double to about
  `2⁻⁹⁵`, a rounding test on the result, and a 320-bit fixed-point `BigInteger` slow path where the test cannot
  decide (the result within the error bound of a midpoint) or the double-double scaling withdraws (a subnormal or
  near-overflow result).

five to eight times the pinned build on .NET and twelve to thirty-seven times under node.

## Error against the reference

| function | n | candidate | worst ulps | mean ulps | 0 | 1 | 2 | >2 |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| exp | 3320 | faithful-pinned | 1 | 0.0934 | 3010 | 310 | 0 | 0 |
| exp | 3320 | correctly rounded | 0 | 0.0000 | 3320 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| ln | 3107 | faithful-pinned | 1 | 0.0100 | 3076 | 31 | 0 | 0 |
| ln | 3107 | correctly rounded | 1 | 0.0003 | 3106 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| log10 | 3107 | faithful-pinned | 1 | 0.0763 | 2870 | 237 | 0 | 0 |
| log10 | 3107 | correctly rounded | 1 | 0.0029 | 3098 | 9 | 0 | 0 |
| pow | 3847 | faithful-pinned | 1 | 0.1032 | 3450 | 397 | 0 | 0 |
| pow | 3847 | correctly rounded | 1 | 0.0218 | 3763 | 84 | 0 | 0 |

The figures are identical from the .NET and the node capture. The pinned build is within one ulp of the true
value on every sample. The prototype is correctly rounded on `exp` and NOT yet on the other three: its sloppy
double-double sum and the `2⁻⁹⁵` bound it assumes are the likely cause, and closing that gap is more of exactly
the work that separates the candidates. Its first run was caught at 256 ulps on `ln` just below one — a
cancellation between `e · ln 2` and `ln m` that the fold into `[√2/2, √2)` now avoids — which is the instrument
seeing a difference, as required.

## Byte identity

`BYTE IDENTITY: faithful-pinned answers differ on 0 of 13381 lines` — the .NET and node captures are identical
in every input and every answer, both candidates' columns included.

## Lines per twin

- Faithful-pinned: `Elementary.fs` is 436 lines with its comments — about forty-five for `exp`, fifty for `ln`,
  fifteen for `log10`, a hundred and ten for `pow` (sixty of them the double-double helpers), the table of the
  powers of two built in a loop, and D43's table as data. Twenty-one published coefficients and six split
  constants.
- Correctly rounded: about three hundred further lines in the harness (`DD`, `Big`, `CorrectlyRounded`), plus a
  multiprecision integer in every twin — JavaScript, Python and .NET have one; C, Rust, Go, Swift and Kotlin
  take a dependency or write one.

## The decision

The faithful-pinned build ships (`DECISIONS.md` D52 item 1): it answers the question the plan's D6 asked — the
same bits on every host — within one ulp of the truth at the cost of the host's own libm, where the correctly
rounded candidate costs five to twenty-five times as much per value, tens of microseconds on its slow path, a
prototype that is not yet correct, and a multiprecision dependency in five twins, for agreement with other
correctly rounded libraries that nothing downstream reads.
