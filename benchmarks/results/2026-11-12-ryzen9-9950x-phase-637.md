# Compute benchmarks — 2026-11-12, AMD Ryzen 9 9950X, Phase 637 (the cost of calling the matrix kernels across the package boundary)

Phase 632 moved `Glm` and least squares into `Fuaran.Compute.Regression`; `Kernels.canonical` and `Kernels.lend` lost
`inline` because F# cannot inline an `internal` value into another assembly (`DECISIONS.md` D75). This file records what
Regression pays for that: the same tree timed as shipped and with both kernels inlined at Regression's call sites.

**Result: the difference is below the run-to-run spread on both hosts, so no source changes** (D75 gains a sentence).

## How it was taken

- Machine: AMD Ryzen 9 9950X (16 cores, 32 threads), Windows 11; .NET SDK 10.0.203 (Release, x64), node v22.23.4
  (Fable 5.0.0, the repository's tool manifest). The machine was not quiet during the timings.
- Harness: `benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/BoundaryBench.fs` (new), the node leg's program, one program on both
  hosts: `boundary` family, `dotnet <Node.dll> 15 boundary` and `node Program.js 15 boundary`. Phase 632 recorded no
  benchmark sizes of its own (it moved code and measured nothing), so the sizes are this phase's: 100, 10,000 and
  100,000 rows, four predictors and an intercept. Five cases per size:
  - `solve`: `LeastSquares.solve` over a dense design (OLS, called directly);
  - `glm poisson`: `Glm.fit` Poisson under the log link (IRLS, called directly);
  - `Fit ols`, `Fit poisson`, `Predict ols`: the DataFrame verbs over a table (`Predict` fits over the training rows and
    scores them), which add the evaluator.
- Every input is built before the clock starts. A case is warmed by a fixed count of calls (2,000, 30 and 5 for 100,
  10,000 and 100,000 rows), then 15 samples of a fixed count of calls (1,000, 20 and 1) are timed; a process reports the
  median of its 15 samples, microseconds per call. The node clock is `DateTime` at a millisecond.
- Two builds of one tree, both copied outside the worktree (to `C:\w637s` and `C:\w637i`, a short path, so the build is
  clear of MAX_PATH) and neither committed:
  - `shipped`: the tree as it stands;
  - `inlined`: the same tree with `Kernels.canonical` and `Kernels.lend` replaced, at every one of Regression's call
    sites (`LeastSquares.fs`, `Glm.fs`; 14 of them), by `inline` copies in an `InlK` module of Regression. The probe was
    checked, not assumed: the `shipped` Regression assembly holds member references to `canonical` and `lend` and the
    `inlined` one holds none; the Fable output of `LeastSquares` imports `Kernels_canonical` and `Kernels_lend` in the one
    and neither in the other. `Kernels.adopt` stays a call in both (it was never `inline`).
- Seven processes per configuration per host, alternating which configuration goes first each round, so a drift of the
  machine falls on both. The cell is the median of the seven processes' medians, with the smallest and largest of the
  seven beside it. "Below the spread": the two configurations' medians differ by less than the larger of the two
  configurations' ranges.

## Reading it

- Every one of the 30 cells is below the spread. On .NET the geometric mean of the 15 ratios is 0.996 (6 of 15 above 1;
  extremes 0.967 and 1.056); the two configurations' ranges are wider than any difference between their medians.
- Under node the geometric mean is 1.023 in favour of the inlined build, 12 of 15 above 1, extremes 0.986 and 1.062. That
  is a few per cent at a one-millisecond clock, inside each configuration's own range (the widest from other processes
  on a shared machine), so it is a direction and not a finding.
- The count of calls explains it: at Regression's call sites `canonical` is reached once per result scalar and `lend` once
  per fold call or per input vector, a number that does not grow with the row count, against O(n·p²) work in each
  iteration. At 100 rows `solve` takes about 13 µs on .NET and the boundary does not show there either.
