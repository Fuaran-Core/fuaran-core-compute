# Compute benchmarks — 2026-10-04, Intel Core i7-9700, Phase 342 (an evaluation whose result stays prepared)

Phase 342 adds `DataFrame.evalToPrepared`: `evalPrepared` with the boundary out not paid, answering a
`Prepared` whose table is built only if `toTable` is called. This file records what that buys a
consumer that chains pipelines — three in sequence over the sheet's `orders` at 1,000, 10,000 and
100,000 rows — through the `Table` boundary at every hop against kept prepared between hops.

## Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The kept result against `evalPrepared` | **Equal**, table and errors, on both hosts: the prepared-result law (`tests/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Tests/PreparedResultLaw.fs`) green over the transform vectors' sample on .NET and under node (1,927 comparisons, every vector and every vector's kept result fed every pipeline of the sample as a follow-on), and the two arms below asserted equal as canonical wire at every size before anything was timed |
| Prepared-chained against Table-chained at 100,000 rows, .NET | **0.38 of the time** (29.9 ms against 79.5 ms), **0.48 of the bytes** (19.0 MB against 40.0 MB) |
| The same, node | **0.58 of the time** (112.5 ms against 193.0 ms) |
| At 1,000 rows | 0.54 of the time on .NET, 0.72 under node |
| Is the boundary paid once | **Yes**: the prepared chain pays one `prepare` in and one table out; the Table chain pays three of each |

## The chain

`Corpus.chainPipelines`, each reading the one before's answer, as a sheet whose nodes feed each other:

1. `lines` — `amount = qty * price`, `big = amount >= threshold` (every row kept);
2. `Filter big`, then `net = amount * 0.8` (about two fifths of the rows; the answer leaves a
   selection, so the kept result is gathered dense — one typed gather per column);
3. `tax = amount - net`, then a projection to `id, region, net, tax`.

**Table-chained** (`Corpus.chainViaTables`): `evalPipelineInEnv` three times, each answer built as a
`Table` and unpacked by the next. **Prepared-chained** (`Corpus.chainViaPrepared`): `prepare orders`,
`evalToPrepared` for the first two, `evalPrepared` for the last. Both start from the same `Table` and
end with the same `Table`; the first `prepare` and the last table are inside the timed region on both
arms.

## What the figures say

The saving is the boundary itself: two answers not built as `Cell list` columns and two tables not
unpacked again, against two dense gathers of the filtered hop. It grows with the rows on both hosts,
because the boundary is linear in cells while the chain's per-call overhead is not. node gains less
than .NET in proportion because its evaluation steps are dearer relative to the boundary (Phase 327's
`toTable lines` at 100,000 rows: 9 to 12 ms on .NET, 48 ms under node, against a chain that costs 80
and 193 ms here).

The prepared chain still pays the boundary twice: `prepare` on the way in, and the last table on the
way out. A consumer whose source is already prepared, or whose last consumer reads a prepared source,
pays less than shown.
