# The join's overhead (D60), and a correction to the figure that prompted it

x64, 32 logical processors, Windows 11, .NET 10, Release, `instrument: X64 process on a X64 machine`,
BenchmarkDotNet v0.15.8. The machine was not isolated, so the times are approximate. The allocations are
stable and are the stronger evidence.

## Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Was the join 9 times a hand-written hash join, as `2026-11-07-x64-date-comparison.md` first said? | No. That harness rebuilt the right table inside the timed run. Measured properly it was about 2.4 times at 100,000 rows |
| Where was the removable cost? | A second table over the codes of a single key, growing output lists, and null handling on a side with no padded row |
| What did removing it buy? | A third off the time and the bytes at 100,000 rows, with no answer changed |

## The int join, `*Typed*` (Inner, `evalPipeline`, one match per row)

| Rows | Before | After | Before, bytes | After, bytes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 34.2 µs | 22.9 µs | 182 KB | 148 KB |

## Against the hand arm

`Typed` has no hand arm, so the hand-written hash join (a presized `Dictionary`, gathering all five output
columns) was timed in a scratch harness outside this repository: a 3-second warm-up per arm, then the median of
201 runs, each size in its own process. The ratios below divide the table above by those medians, so they cross
two harnesses and are approximate.

| Rows | Hand arm | Hand, bytes | Ratio before | Ratio after |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | about 15 µs | 61 KB | 2.3 | 1.5 |
| 10,000 | about 90 µs | 588 KB | 4.8 | 3.7 |
| 100,000 | about 1,550 µs | 6,028 KB | 2.4 | 1.6 |

The same harness, evaluator medians only, old and new code side by side:

| Rows | Inner | Outer | Semi |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 29 to 20 µs | 29 to 22 µs | 22 to 15 µs |
| 10,000 | 346-454 to 240 µs | 340-353 to 245 µs | 255-266 to 192 µs |
| 100,000 | 3,230-3,520 to 2,070-2,290 µs | 3,170-3,440 to 2,300-2,610 µs | 2,170-2,230 to 1,320-1,420 µs |

One run of the new code's Inner at 1,000 rows read 28.6 µs against 19.5 µs in the other; the BenchmarkDotNet
figure above agrees with the lower.

## The harness traps behind the wrong figure

- **The input was built inside the timed closure.** `Corpus.typedPipeline "join"` embeds the right table, so
  calling it per run timed the table's construction too. Doing so again reproduces 9.8 ms and 22.7 MB, the
  figure first reported, against 3.0 ms and 10.7 MB for the join alone.
- **A count of warm-up runs is not a warm-up.** 300 runs at 1,000 rows last about 20 ms, shorter than the
  runtime's tiering delay, so they time unoptimised code: 98 µs where the warmed figure is 29 µs. It also made
  the new code look slower at 1,000 rows in a mixed-size process, which separate processes and a timed warm-up
  did not bear out.

## Still open

- **10,000 rows is the join's worst ratio.** The sample's key values span 100,000, past the direct table's
  limit of four times the rows, so this size codes through a `Dictionary` that grows from empty. Presizing it
  is not a free change: `codesOf` is shared with the group-by, where the rows say little about the cardinality.
- **The dated pipeline's tail, not the join's.** `byMonth` at 100,000 rows has a median of 850-900 µs (about 1.7
  times the hand arm's 520 µs) but a mean of 3,100-4,200 µs and a p95 near 14.7 ms, so the 4.8 ms and the 9.2
  ratio in `2026-11-07-x64-date-comparison.md` are a mean over a tail. With `DOTNET_gcConcurrent=0` the median
  is 2,210 µs, the mean 2,404 µs and the p95 4,000 µs; with `DOTNET_PROCESSOR_COUNT=1` they are 3,432, 4,090 and
  6,779 µs. The garbage collector's mode moves it, and 40-50 gen2 collections fell in 201 runs where the hand arm
  had none. The cause is not established.
