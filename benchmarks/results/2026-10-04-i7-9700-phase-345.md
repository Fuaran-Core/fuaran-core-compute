## What was measured

**The algorithm** is CPython's `math.fsum` (`Modules/mathmodule.c`, `math_fsum_impl`): Shewchuk's
non-overlapping partials, with CPython's final collapse that rounds the partials to the nearest
double, half-way cases decided by the sign of the next partial. It is exactly rounded: the answer is
the double nearest the true (infinitely precise) sum, so it cannot depend on the order or
partitioning of the additions. Python ships it; TypeScript, Go and Rust would carry the ~40 lines in
`bench.mjs` / `Program.fs`. **The fold** is today's left-to-right `+` from `0.0`.

**Three inputs**, drawn from one xorshift64\* stream so every leg sums the same bytes:

- `quarters` — whole quarters in `[0, 1000)`, the shape of the benchmark corpus's `v` column. Every
  partial sum is exact, so the fold and the exact sum agree to the bit; this is the exact sum's cost
  when it buys nothing.
- `uniform` — uniform doubles in `[0, 1)`: the ordinary case, two partials.
- `wide` — mixed-sign values spanning `1e-8` to `1e8`: the adversarial case, seven to nine partials.

**Reading it.** Per value the fold costs 1.3 ns on both hosts. The exact sum costs 5 ns where the
inputs are exact, 8 ns at ordinary dynamic range and 38 to 43 ns at wide range: the inner loop runs
once per live partial, and the partial count is set by the input's spread of magnitudes, not by `n`.
The two hosts agree on the ratio, because both run the same scalar loop. Eight ranges on the .NET
thread pool bring the exact sum to 1.9x the fold at a million ordinary values and under the fold
at a million exact ones, 8.5x at wide range; at 10,000 values the pool's dispatch is most of the time.

**What the fold's share of a real step is.** D7 item 3 times the whole streamed grouping — key
slots, member counts, and the float `Sum` fold — at 3.94 ms for a million rows under ten keys. The
exact sum adds 7 ms to that step at ordinary range (a 2.8x slower `GroupBy`) and 42 ms at wide range
(11x), single-threaded; split eight ways, 1.2 ms and 9.5 ms. A `Mean` pays the same; a `StdDev`
pays it once for the mean and, if its squares are summed exactly too, once more.

## The sum is order- and partition-invariant, and the three hosts agree to the bit

For every input, the .NET leg checked that the exact sum of five shuffles of the input, of the input
split into 3, 5, 8, 64 and 1,000 ranges (each range's partials fed through the same algorithm), and
of the eight-range pool run, all have the bit pattern of the sequential exact sum. All 45 checks
held. The fold does not: the shuffled fold differs from the in-order fold on every non-exact input.

had the algorithm been order-dependent, or had a host's `+` or `abs` differed, a cell would differ,
and the fold columns show what a difference looks like. The in-order fold differs from the exact sum
by 1 to 230 ulps here (`uniform`, a million values: `…2585` against `…249f`).

## How many transform law vectors change bytes: none

`conformance/laws/transform-laws.json` (kitVersion 0.38.0, 60 vectors) holds three float summations:
`mean` over `w` in `transform-3-group-agg` (groups `{3, 4.5}`, `{3.5}`, `{4}`) and in
`transform-11-group-agg` (three singletons), and no float `sum`, `stddev`, `cumulSum` or rolling
window (the `sum` aggregates are over `int` and `decimal` columns; `transform-25`'s `cumulSum` is
decimal). Every one of those sums is exact in binary, so the fold and the exact sum agree to the bit
and **no vector moves**. The corpus as it stands cannot tell the two summations apart — a host that
implemented either would certify. A law for this phase would need vectors whose members are not
exactly summable (`0.1`-class values, three or more members).

## The twins' cost to follow

Per Phase 356's finding (D6): the only host running the transform-laws family is `fuaran-ts`;
`fuaran-rs` (`src/transform/`) and `fuaran-go` (`internal/core/dataframe`) carry evaluator twins with
no laws leg; `fuaran-py` has no evaluator twin. So the change-set on a yes is: the algorithm above in
TypeScript (`bench.mjs` is it), Go and Rust (a direct port; Python would call `math.fsum`), the
corpus vectors re-emitted with members that exercise it, and `fuaran-ts`'s laws leg green — on top of
the decimal strand it still lacks (fuaran#2001), which is what blocks the corpus copy today.
