# Compute benchmarks — 2026-10-03, Intel Core i7-8650U, Phase 357 (a compact form for the incremental state's wire)

Phase 355 gave `IncrementalEval` a wire form and measured it
(`2026-10-03-i7-8650u-phase-355.md`): at 1,000,000 rows on .NET a decode took 11.8 to 12.6 s carrying
its source (6.4 to 7.3 s detached) against 0.09 to 0.38 s for a full evaluation. Phase 357 replaces
that form's one JSON value per cell with typed runs and measures the result on the same machine, the
same pipelines and the same harness.

## Where 355's time went, before the form was chosen

Taken on 355's tree, Release build, at 1,000,000 rows of `filter-groupby` and `filter-sort-limit`,
each piece timed on its own (best of two) and the remainder attributed to the walk. Run under `dotnet
fsi` against the Release assemblies rather than in the harness, so the totals read higher and noisier
than the harness's (a collection over a multi-gigabyte heap lands in whichever piece is running); the
proportions are what was used.

| Piece | `filter-groupby` (ms) | `filter-sort-limit` (ms) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Decode, whole | 11,751 | 7,386 |
| the digest over the body (52 and 57 million characters) | 1,669 | 2,507 |
| the parse into `JVal` | 3,883 | 4,065 |
| the walk from `JVal` into cells, tokens and indexes, and the checks | 6,199 | 814 |
| Decode over a supplied source, whole | 8,923 | 5,522 |
| the source's fingerprint (render through `JVal`, then the digest) | 2,707 | 1,938 |
| Encode, whole | 20,707 | 13,597 |
| the source and the members built as `JVal` | 1,998 | 452 |
| their canonical render | 6,179 | 5,918 |
| the digests (the source's, then the body's) | 2,438 | 3,512 |

Three costs, then, in the order they matter:

- **One `JVal` per cell.** The parse allocates a value and a list cell for each of some 8 to 10
  million values, and the walk visits each again to make the cell; the encode builds the same values
  and walks them to render. This is the cost the phase was filed for.
- **The digest.** The package's SHA-256 copy runs at about 30 ns a character on .NET (a closure call
  per byte), which is 15 to 35 per cent of a decode and the whole of a fingerprint's tail.
- **The source's fingerprint** renders the source through `JVal` again where the encoding already
  holds it as text.

## The target, stated before the work

The decode at 1,000,000 rows on .NET is expected to come down **at least threefold**: from 11.8 to
12.6 s carrying the source to **4 s or less**, and from 6.4 to 7.3 s detached to **2.5 s or less**.
The expectation behind the target is nearer 2 to 3 s carrying: the runs remove the per-cell values
from the parse and the walk, and the digest on .NET goes to the platform's SHA-256 (the managed copy
stays the Fable path, value-identical). What cannot move in this phase is the substrate's JSON parser
itself, which still reads every character of a run's string once into a `StringBuilder`, and the
cells and lists the state is made of, which a decode has to build whatever the wire holds. If the
reduction falls short of threefold, it is one of those two, and this file says which.
