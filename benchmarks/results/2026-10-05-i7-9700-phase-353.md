# Compute benchmarks — 2026-10-05, Intel Core i7-9700, Phase 353 (the pivot per pair, and the `lines` boundary)

Phase 326 left two node figures above their bars and named a cause for each: the pivot at 1.34 to 1.43
times its Phase 267 row-frame estimate, with 16 per cent of its profile in union equality inside
`Column.aggregate`; and `lines` at 100,000 rows at 0.50 to 0.74 of 8a39a42, with "a third of that
run's profile" attributed to the garbage collector handling 200,000 boxed output cells. This phase
measured both again on the tree it started from (c4674f7) before changing anything. The first
attribution held. The second did not.

## Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The pivot on node | **0.42 to 0.44 of its row-frame estimate** (c4674f7 reads 1.08 to 1.17 in the same rotations). Every (group, on-value) pair is now aggregated by Phase 323's streams — one per pivot column, a slot per group — not by a boxed cell list and a `Column.aggregate` call per pair |
| `Column.aggregate` itself | **A defect, fixed in Core** (`fuaran-core` b14dbd0, riding Core's `0.35.0` draft, class none). It tested its aggregate and column type with a union's `=` about a dozen times per call and twice per cell, and `ColumnType.widens` three times per cell; under Fable each is a structural-equality call. Now pattern matches: 5,000 one-cell `Sum`s fall from 5.3 to 0.85 ms under node, the same answers on both hosts. This repository pins Core `0.34.0`, so the pairs a stream defers (`Median`, `StdDev`, `CountDistinct`, the refusals and recomputations) take the faster path when the pin rises; the streamed pairs do not need it |
| `lines` at 100,000 rows on node | **0.26 to 0.35 of 8a39a42** (best of seven; 0.29 to 0.36 by median). At 1,000 rows 0.25 to 0.26, at 10,000 0.24 to 0.25 |
| What the `lines` time was | **Not boxing.** On c4674f7 the profile is 31 per cent `Array.setItem`, every sample of it under `Frame.physical`: the identity selection, rebuilt by `Array.init` (one bounds-checked `setItem` per row under Fable) once per `Derive` and once more at the boundary out. The garbage collector is 12 per cent of the whole run — the boundary in, the evaluation and the boundary out together. `physical` now fills the identity with a counted loop of direct writes |
| The output cells, counted | `lines` returns six columns. Four are handed back as the consumer's own lists (the boundary's origins). `big` is `Bool`s, which are interned (Phase 327). Only `amount` boxes a fresh cell per row: **100,000 `Float` cells, not 200,000**. The other 200,000 allocations at the boundary are the list nodes of `amount` and `big`, which ARE the public `Table` shape (`Cell list`) |
| Sharing repeated float cells | **Built, measured, and not kept.** A per-column direct-mapped table of `Float` cells at `toTable` (equal values, `-0` apart, share one cell; `amount`'s 100,000 values take at most 7,940 distinct ones) moved the collector from 11.8 to 10.4 per cent of the profile and the wall clock not at all (`lines` 100,000: full 66 to 75 ms against 66 to 74; prepared-then-`toTable` 48 to 49 against 47 to 48). It is not shipped |
| A typed output path | **Not built.** With the boundary out at about 14 ms of a 66 ms run, and the boxed cells a fraction of that, an opt-in typed read would buy a consumer less than the identity fix did for everyone, and it is new public surface. If a later profile shows the list shape itself dominating, the proposal is a `Table` whose column carries an array (or a typed vector) beside, or instead of, its `Cell list` — a public `Table` change, out of this phase's scope |
| Byte identity, node against .NET | **Identical.** The 47 `transform-laws.json` vectors (`TransformVectorSample`, reproduced on each host) digest the same on node and .NET, on c4674f7 and on the phase's tree; so do the corpus answers of `lines` (three sizes), `byRegion` (two), the 50-on-value pivot under `Sum`, `Median`, `Count` and `Mean` |
| The pivot's semantics | Held to the per-pair `Column.aggregate` answer by a new differential law (`pivotStreamLaws`, 3,000 drawn tables, every aggregate, two index shapes, numeric on-values whose `cellEq` merges what the token separates and string ones). The law is green on the pre-phase evaluator and on this one, and red against two perturbed accumulators (`MaxKeepsFirstTie`, `CountCountsNulls`) |
| The tick family | The suite's Release clock leg, in the gate |

## Instrument

node v25.9.0, the sources compiled by Fable 5.0.0, on an 8-core i7-9700 (Windows 11). A probe over the
benchmark corpus (`Corpus.fs`) through the public entry points (`DataFrame.evalPipelineInEnv`, and for
the breakdown `DataFrame.evalToPrepared` and `DataFrame.toTable`): two warm-up calls, calls per sample
doubled until a sample spans 50 ms, best and median of seven samples. Three trees, each exported whole
and compiled alone: 8a39a42 (the bar `lines` and the pivot's estimate are set against), c4674f7 (this
phase's base) and the phase's tree, run in rotation, three times. The answer digest is a polynomial
hash of the answer's canonical wire encoding carried in a double below 2^53, so it reads the same digits
on both hosts. Compiled for .NET (Release) the same probe is the .NET leg. Profiles by
`node --cpu-prof` over the `lines` family, self time.

As in the Phase 326 file, the pivot's row-frame figure on this machine is estimated through 8a39a42:
its figure divided by Phase 267's after/before ratio for the pivot, 1.36.

Phase 353 against the estimate: **0.42 to 0.44**; c4674f7 against it, 1.08 to 1.17. Before the identity fix below landed, the streams
alone read 4.9 to 5.3 ms against c4674f7's 11.0 to 13.0 in the same rotations.

The other aggregates, c4674f7 against the phase's tree, two rotations: `Count` 9.1 to 9.5 against 3.1
to 3.3, `Mean` 8.5 to 8.6 against 3.7 to 4.1, and `Median` — which every pair defers — 10.5 to 10.9
against 9.4 to 9.5. A deferred pair is aggregated exactly as before; what moved for `Median` is the
fill loop it no longer runs (an aggregate every stream defers is fed nothing), the counted loop that
builds its member list, and the identity fix. The rest of `Median`'s time is `Column.aggregate`, which
the Core fix above reaches when this repository's pin rises.

The breakdown at 100,000 rows, best of seven: the evaluation alone over a prepared source (no boundary
either way) 29 to 31 ms on c4674f7 and 15.5 to 16 on the phase's tree; with the boundary out
(`toTable`) added, 42 to 46 against 19.5 to 22.5. Both halves moved by the same cause: each `Derive`
and the boundary out asked for the identity selection, and each paid 100,000 bounds-checked writes
for it.

Profile, `lines` family, self time: c4674f7 — `setItem` 31.1 per cent (24.2 under the derives'
`physical`, 6.5 under `toTable`'s), garbage collector 11.8; the phase's tree — garbage collector 12.7,
no `setItem` above 1 per cent, the largest remaining entries the derive's own evaluation.

`byRegion`, for the same rotations: at 100,000 rows 162 to 170 ms on 8a39a42, 36.5 to 37.5 on c4674f7,
22.0 to 22.5 on the phase's tree; at 1,000 rows 1.59 to 1.81, 0.41 to 0.45, 0.27.

## .NET

Unchanged answers, and no regression the machine's noise can show: the pivot falls from 2.4 to 3.9 ms
to 0.73 to 0.87 (the streams), `byRegion` and `lines` read within the run-to-run spread (a .NET
`Array.init` and a counted loop of writes are the same work there). `Column.aggregate` over one-cell
and twenty-cell columns reads level before and after the Core fix.
