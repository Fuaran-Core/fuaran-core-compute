# Compute benchmarks — 2026-11-24, Intel Core i7-8650U, Phase 730 (the geometric algebra's products)

Phase 730 asks whether a product written for the coefficients its operands' types carry is worth having beside the
general product of `Fuaran.Compute.GeometricAlgebra`, which folds all 1,024 signed terms of `DECISIONS.md` D85 item 3
whatever its operands hold. This file records the measurement the decision rests on (`DECISIONS.md` D89). Its first
part, the general product alone, was taken and committed before any specialised product was written; the second, the
specialised products beside it, on the final tree, after D89 had stated the margin they had to clear.

## How it was taken

- Machine: Intel Core i7-8650U (4 cores, AVX2), Windows 11, on mains power. A laptop that was running other work:
  no gate ran during any measurement (the gate queue was checked empty first), but the machine was not otherwise
  quiet, and the spread below shows it.
- .NET: SDK 10.0.404 (the repository's `global.json` pins 10.0.203 with `rollForward: latestFeature`), runtime
  Microsoft.NETCore.App 10.0.15, Release build, x64 native process.
- node v22.20.0; the JavaScript is Fable 5.0.0's, from the repository's own tool manifest.
- Harness: the node leg's timing program (`benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node`), family
  `geometric-algebra` (`../GeometricAlgebraBench.fs`), the same F# program on both hosts:
  - .NET: `dotnet build -c Release benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node`, then
    `dotnet benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node/bin/Release/net10.0/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks.Node.dll 11 geometric-algebra`,
    three times;
  - node: `pwsh benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node/run-node.ps1 -FableFrom . -Runs 11 -Only geometric-algebra -KeepOutput`
    for the first run, which compiles with `dotnet fable <Node project> -o <out> --noCache` and runs
    `node <out>/benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node/Program.js 11 geometric-algebra`; the second and
    third runs are that `node` command again over the kept output.
- The sample: 1,000 operands of each type drawn at seed 730 through the pinned Park–Miller generator, so the same
  operands on both hosts: points `Point.up` of coordinates in `[−10, 10)`, spheres `Sphere.ofCentreRadius` of radius
  in `[0.5, 5)`, rotors `Rotor.ofAxisAngle` of angle in `[−3, 3)`, translators `Translator.ofVector`, and motors
  `Motor.ofRotorTranslator`. Every product operand is a typed value widened by its own `toMultivector`, before the
  clock starts.
- Each case: Phase 593's timer (`ElementaryBench.perValue`) with its samples kept — at least half a second of warm-up,
  then passes over the sample doubled until one spans fifty milliseconds, then 11 samples. Each row gives the median,
  the least and the greatest sample, in nanoseconds per call. Every run is reported, not the best.
- The checksum column is the bit pattern of the float sum of one coefficient of every answer. It is the same in every
  run on both hosts, row for row, so both hosts computed the same bits over the sample.
- The raw captures are in `2026-11-24-i7-8650u-phase-730/` beside this file, one per run and host, as printed but for
  the line the node script prints last, which names the local directory it kept the JavaScript in.

What it says:

- On .NET the geometric product costs about two microseconds on every shape, a point with a sphere as much as a motor
  with a motor: it folds the same 1,024 terms whatever its operands hold, so the operands' shape changes nothing.
  The outer product, 243 terms, costs about a quarter of that, and the scalar product, 32 terms, about a twentieth.
- A typed function costs its products and little else: a composition is one product, an application two.
- Under node every product costs about fifty times what it costs on .NET, about a hundred microseconds for the
  geometric product, and a typed function's cost is again its products.
- The spread is wide. Within one run the greatest sample is up to 1.06 times the median above the least on .NET and
  1.50 times under node; across the three runs one case's median moved by a factor of up to 2.34 on .NET
  (`Motor.compose`, 2023 to 4739) and 2.09 under node (`Sphere.power`, 11125 to 23250). A gain smaller than that
  could be noise on this machine. The margin D89 states is chosen from it.

## The specialised products, after

`DECISIONS.md` D89 stated its margin before any of these was taken (`42bb282`): a specialised product is kept only
where, on both hosts, the greatest of its three run medians is below the least of the general product's three run
medians on the same operands divided by 2.5. Taken on the final tree (`73f765d`), by the commands above, three runs on
each host; the general product's medians are from the same runs, on the same operands, beside each specialised one.
Every figure in this section is computed from the committed captures by `figures.py` beside this file
(`python benchmarks/results/2026-11-24-i7-8650u-phase-730/figures.py`), not read by eye, except the script-host
figures in its last part (4.5 to 4.6 µs, 2.0 to 2.4 µs and 4.6 µs), which are reported from runs that were not
captured to files and cannot be recomputed.

### The same captures, read other ways

- **Against the baseline runs' general medians**, for the seven pairs the baseline measured: the least ratio is 3.85 on
  .NET (`(rotor point) * reverse rotor`, 1826 over 474) and 5.32 under node (`(motor point) * reverse motor`). The
  baseline did not measure the two translator-sandwich pairs: their general figures come only from the final tree's
  runs above.
- **Pooled over every committed default-configuration run of the final tree**, on .NET the three runs above and the
  four `pgo-final-*` runs (seven; those four took 5 samples a case, by the same timer), under node the three above:
  eight pairs clear 2.5 on both hosts; **one does not: `(motor point) * reverse motor` on .NET, 2.08** (the least general median, 2207, over the greatest specialised one,
  1059, in `pgo-final-1`). Under node it pools at 5.42.
- **In one process** (the general median over the specialised median of the same run), the least over the pooled runs
  is 4.35 on .NET (`(motor point) * reverse motor`, `pgo-final-1`) and 6.24 under node (the same pair,
  `specialised-node-3`); every other pair's least is 8.17 or more on .NET and 12.12 or more under node.
- **The specialised fold has a slow mode of its own on .NET.** `(motor point) * reverse motor`: 412 to 466 ns in six of
  the seven pooled runs, 1059 ns in `pgo-final-1`, and 814 ns in `interim-cfe5cdb-dotnet-3` on an intermediate tree.
  `(rotor point) * reverse rotor`: 228 to 264 ns in five, 474 and 484 ns in two. `translator * point`: 177 to 189 ns in
  six, 337 ns in one. The cause is not established.
- **The general product's own spread on the final tree exceeds the margin on .NET.** Its median for `motor * motor`
  moved from 2189 to 7471 ns across the three runs above, a factor of 3.41, and pooled over seven runs
  `(rotor point) * reverse rotor`'s moved by 3.53; under node the largest factor is 1.82. A ratio taken across runs,
  as the rule takes it, can therefore move by more than the margin with the machine alone on .NET; the same-process
  ratios do not mix two processes.
- **Bits.** In every pooled run on both hosts each specialised arm's checksum is its general arm's, and every row's
  checksum is the baseline's.

### A function no pair serves, on .NET

Every committed .NET run of each tree, the same operands throughout:

- `Motor.applyToSphere`: 4338 to 5323 ns in all seven runs of the base tree (`general-dotnet-*`, `pgo-base-*`);
  9388 to 12045 ns in five of the seven runs of the final tree, and 4664 and 4880 ns in the other two
  (`specialised-dotnet-*`, `pgo-final-*`).
- The general product on `point * sphere`: 1754 to 2586 ns in all seven base runs; 4361 to 4747 ns in six of the seven
  final runs, and 2537 ns in the other.
- With dynamic profile-guided optimisation off (`DOTNET_TieredPGO=0`, `nopgo-*`, two runs) or tiered compilation off
  (`DOTNET_TieredCompilation=0`, `notier-1`), the final tree ran the general product at 2043 to 2240 ns and
  `Motor.applyToSphere` at 4403 to 4626 ns. The base tree was not measured in those configurations.
- The final tree with the general fold marked `AggressiveOptimization` (`aggr-*`, not kept) ran the general product at
  4374 to 7190 ns.
- Outside this harness, in a script host (`dotnet fsi --optimize+`, the release assembly, 200,000 products of two fixed
  multivectors), the base tree ran the general product at 4.5 to 4.6 µs in three processes of three, and this phase's
  trees at 2.0 to 2.4 µs in two of three (4.6 in the third). Those runs were not captured to files.

So in this harness a function no pair serves measured slower on .NET on the final tree in five runs of seven, against
none of seven on the base, and this phase has not established why, nor whether the same holds in another host or on
another machine.
