# Compute benchmarks — 2026-10-02, Intel Core i7-8650U, Phase 327 (the Table boundary)

Phase 327 set out to lower the floor of the `Table` boundary in both directions: `Frame.ofTable`
packing each column straight from its `Cell list` in one walk (no intermediate `Cell[]`), and
`toTable` / `cellAt` handing back shared `Bool` cells and shared `Int` cells for a small range rather
than a fresh cell per read. Both were built and measured. **Only the second ships.** The direct pack
met its own target and is deliberately not taken, because of what it does to the tick family; the
reason is the first section below.

## Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Prepare at 100,000 rows by four columns, .NET, the direct pack | **4.25 ms and 2.80 MB**, against 11.14 ms and 6.00 MB on 8a39a42 and 13.12 ms on the pre-phase tree (99e0a1d) in the same paired run: 2.6x faster, under half, as the shard asked. **Built, measured, not taken** |
| The same on node (Fable) | **21.5 ms**, against 34.0 ms on 8a39a42 and 37.0 ms on 99e0a1d (1.6x). Not taken |
| Why not taken | The table-fed tick (`Delta.diff` plus the refresh) does not cross this boundary, so a cheaper unpack lowers only the FULL evaluation the tick is held against. Measured, the tick did not move and the full did, so every tick ratio roughly doubled: see "The direct pack and the tick family" |
| Boundary-out allocation, `lines` read back at 100,000 rows (interning, shipped) | **10,000,568 B against 12,400,548 B** (down 19.4%, past the sixth the shard asked for); best time 8.7 ms against 11.9 ms in the same paired run |
| The same with every column boxed (no origin list to hand back) | `lines` 31.6 MB against 36.4 MB (down 13%); `orders` 22.0 MB against 24.4 MB (down 10%) |
| node, interning | No change measurable on this machine: `toTable lines` best-of-run 48 to 76 ms with interning against 47 to 85 ms without, over ten paired rounds. node reports no allocation |
| The tick family on the shipped tree | Within the standing bound on every corpus node and every tick case, Release clock leg exit 0 on its first counted attempt, ratios at the pre-phase profile (the interning moves the full evaluation by under 1% of its allocation at 1,000 rows) |
| Corpus answers | Byte-identical: the suite's law vectors and every Table-fed law green; the node harness asserted every corpus agreement (and the decimal-vector law) before timing, on both the direct-pack tree and the shipped tree |

## Instrument

.NET 10 (SDK 10.0.203 band), Release, a console probe compiled once against each of three trees —
8a39a42 (the shard's baseline), 99e0a1d (the pre-phase tree) and the phase's tree — each taken by
`git archive` into a scratch area. The probe is compiled under the assembly name the dataframe
package grants its internals to, so it times `Frame.ofTable` and `Frame.toTable` directly as well as
the public `DataFrame.prepare`. A warm-up of at least 40 calls and 500 ms (enough for tiered
compilation to promote the timed code: a first run with three warm-up calls read the FIRST case at
twice its warm cost on every tree, and was discarded); calls per sample doubled until a sample spans
50 ms; seven samples with a forced GC before each; best and median per call. Allocation is
`GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread` across a sample divided by its calls. The trees were run
**paired and interleaved** over three rounds, and each figure is the tree's best across them.

The machine was **not quiet**: other sessions held it busy throughout. The allocation figures do not
move with load; the times do, and only same-run ratios are claimed. The `orders` table is the sheet
corpus's (`id:int, region:string, qty:int, price:float`); `lines` is the sheet's `lines` node.

The node figures are the same probe compiled by Fable 5.0.0 and run under node v22.20.0, timed with
the wall clock (Fable maps no `Stopwatch` member, as the node harness notes), paired over two to ten
rounds.

The interning column was measured in its own paired run against 99e0a1d (99e0a1d's figures in that
run: prepare 11.228 ms, `toTable lines` 11.879 ms and 12,400,548 B, `lines` Table in and out
30.784 ms and 20,318,276 B). 8a39a42 and 99e0a1d agree byte for byte on every allocation: nothing
between them touched the boundary.

What the direct pack removes is exactly the intermediate array: 32 bytes a row across the four
columns (a 100,000-entry `Cell[]` per column), 3.2 MB at 100,000 rows. What interning removes is one
24-byte cell per present `Bool` and per `Int` in `[-128, 1023]` read back: on `lines` that is the
`big` column (100,000 cells, 2.4 MB); `amount` is a float and `id` mostly past the range.

## The direct pack and the tick family

The clock leg holds the table-fed tick — `Delta.diff` plus `Incremental.refreshOn` over a one-row
edit — within 1.6x of the full evaluation of the new table, on every corpus node and on three
tick cases at 1,000, 20,000 and 100,000 rows. The full evaluation pays `prepare`; the tick does not
(the refresh applies the delta to the state it holds and never unpacks the new table). So a cheaper
`prepare` lowers only the denominator.

Measured with the probe above, 99e0a1d against the direct-pack tree, tick and full timed back to back
per tree (best of seven samples; allocation exact):

| Pipeline | Rows | Tick, 99e0a1d | Tick, direct pack | Full, 99e0a1d | Full, direct pack |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| filter > groupBy | 1,000 | 35.4 KB | 35.6 KB | 0.092 ms, 86,248 B | 0.055 ms, 53,304 B |
| filter > sort > limit | 1,000 | 83.8 KB | 83.8 KB | 0.192 ms, 74,480 B | 0.149 ms, 41,904 B |
| filter > groupBy | 100,000 | 11.6 ms, 2,324,946 B | 11.2 ms, 2,324,946 B | 20.2 ms, 6,830,658 B | 10.5 ms, 3,630,557 B |
| filter > sort > limit | 100,000 | 24.1 ms, 7,533,732 B | 23.8 ms, 7,533,732 B | 39.6 ms, 6,422,908 B | 18.0 ms, 3,222,572 B |
| filter > groupBy > filter | 100,000 | 8.3 ms, 2,327,349 B | 8.7 ms, 2,327,354 B | 16.0 ms, 6,832,228 B | 8.6 ms, 3,632,117 B |

The tick's allocation is identical to the byte and its time unchanged; the full evaluation halves.
On the Release clock leg (three runs of each tree, interleaved):

| Case | 99e0a1d | Direct pack |
|---|---|---|
| tick: filter > sort > limit @ 1,000 | x1.04, x1.02, x0.75 | x1.51, x1.53, x1.45 |
| tick: filter > groupBy @ 100,000 | x0.46, x0.52, x0.52 | x0.92, x1.00, x1.08 |
| tick: filter > sort > limit @ 100,000 | x0.93, x0.89, x0.87 | x1.39, x1.38, x1.37 |
| corpus `lines` @ 100,000 | x0.60, x0.46 | x1.03, x1.13, x1.04 |
| corpus `filter > sort > limit` @ 100,000 | x0.86, x0.88 | x1.15, x1.26, **x1.68** (attempt red, green on retry) |
| corpus `window CumulSum` @ 20,000 | x1.26, x1.33 | x1.45, x1.42, x1.10; the third run **x1.61, x1.78, x1.82** (with x2.08 at 1,000) — red on all three attempts, the leg exited 1 |

On this machine the direct pack left most cases inside the bound and turned the leg red once in
three runs. CI reads these cases with less headroom: on 58eae8d it read `tick: filter > sort > limit`
at 1,000 rows at 1.54x to 1.60x against the 1.6x bound, and a full evaluation 22 to 40 percent
cheaper at that size moves that reading to about 2x. Raising the bound is not the remedy, and the
ratio is not an artefact: it says the tick is worth less against a cheaper full evaluation, which is
true. The direct pack waits until the tick's own floor is lowered (the diff and the refresh are what
remain per row); it is then a self-contained change to `Frame.ofTable` with the measurements above
as its baseline.

These sit at the pre-phase profile: at 1,000 rows the interning changes the full evaluation's

such as `lines`, whose tick ratio has the most room.
