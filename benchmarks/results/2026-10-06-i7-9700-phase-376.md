# Phase 376 — the worker pool over a shared byte-mask frame: what it buys, what it costs, and the sequential member before and after

Intel Core i7-9700, 3.00 GHz, 8 physical cores (no SMT), Windows 11; node v25.9.0 x64; Microsoft Edge
154.0.4258.53 (`--headless=new`); .NET 10 (Release); the evaluator compiled by Fable 5.0.0 from this
tree. One machine; Firefox and Safari were not measured.

What landed (`DECISIONS.md` D14): under Fable the frame's validity masks and `Bools` values are bytes
(`Uint8Array`), each typed vector's carrier and mask share one buffer, and once a host opts in
(`WorkerPool.optIn`) on a realm with shared memory the frame allocates them over a
`SharedArrayBuffer`; a warm, lazily started pool of Web Workers (or node `worker_threads`) reached
only through `DataFrame.evalToPreparedAsync`, the caller draining morsels beside the workers; and a
fallback that is the sequential member byte for byte where the host does not opt in or the page is not
cross-origin isolated. .NET keeps its representation: every accessor there is the plain index, so the
code it runs is the code it ran.

## The laws

Under node (the gate's node leg, Fable 5.0.0, `worker_threads`, every job and reply through the
structured clone `postMessage` makes) and on .NET (workers on the thread pool), with the pool opted
in at a floor of no rows: every step the pool takes answers what `Kernels.oneThread` answers, byte for
byte as canonical wire, refusals included, over the transform vectors' sample at 1, 2, 3 and 8,192
rows a morsel (12 steps each) and the corpus's row-local pipelines at 20,000 rows (14 of 14); and
`evalToPreparedAsync` answers what `evalToPrepared` answers over every whole pipeline of both. Each
with the caller draining beside three workers, and with the workers alone (which proves the workers,
not the caller, answered: 22 to 74 morsels a run under node). A pool whose runners start every morsel
after the first a row late goes red on 11 of 12 answered corpus steps; one that puts each full
morsel's kept rows in its partner's slot (out-of-order concatenation) on 4 of 12 (the filters). Without
isolation (`crossOriginIsolated` false) and without `SharedArrayBuffer`, the opt-in answers `false`,
no pool is active, and the corpus's pipelines answer as `evalToPrepared` does. In Edge: the page served
without the two headers has no `SharedArrayBuffer`, refuses the opt-in, and answers the corpus's six
pipelines as `evalToPrepared` does; served with them it takes the opt-in, and the pool answers the same
six identically. Every pooled timing below was preceded by the same byte comparison.

**Against 346's prototype.** 346's hand-written loops over arrays already in shared memory reached 2.8
to 6.3 times under node, 3.6 to 4.8 times in Edge from a dedicated worker, and 2.5 to 3.1 times from
the page's thread, at 100,000 rows and up. The evaluator itself, through the pool, reaches 3.4 to 5.2
times under node, 3.5 to 4.7 times from a dedicated worker and 3.2 to 4.0 times from the page's thread
over the same range: the prototype's projection that the evaluator's heavier rows would scale at least
as well held. The page's thread at eight threads is again the weak shape (slower than four at a
million-row `filter`, and more than twice the sequential time at 10,000 rows): its waits are message
events on the page's own loop.

**Against 375's single-thread figures.** 375 measured the hand-off's morsels on one thread at what
the sequential member costs; on the pool they divide by the ratios above.

**Where the pool never pays.** At 10,000 rows (two morsels) on every host: faster in 7 of 18 cells
and by at most 14 per cent, and at eight threads on the page's thread 2.1 to 2.2 times SLOWER. At
20,000 rows it is faster in 15 of 18 cells, at 50,000 in all 18. `WorkerPool.PoolRows` is therefore
32,768 rows (four morsels): a smaller step runs on the caller's thread as it always did.

## The pool: start-up and copy, apart

| cost | node | Edge, page thread | Edge, dedicated worker |
|---|---|---|---|
| a cold pool, 1 / 3 / 7 workers importing the evaluator until each reports ready | 137 / 153 / 200 ms | 74 / 110 / 261 ms | 61–65 / 105–146 / 204–255 ms |
| a hand-off's plan, frame in shared memory, a million rows (8 threads; `filter` / `derive`) | 1.59 / 1.82 ms | 1.75 / 1.08 ms | 1.00 / 1.05 ms |
| the copy a frame allocated BEFORE the opt-in costs on top, a million rows | 2.1 / 3.6 ms | 2.3 / 3.9 ms | 2.1 / 3.2 ms |
| the same at 100,000 rows | 0.27 / 0.35 ms | 0.23 / 0.29 ms | 0.25 / 0.27 ms |
| the sequential step over shared memory against plain buffers, a million rows | −2% / −4% | +1% / 0% | −2% / +1% |

Start-up is paid once a page, while the first eligible steps run as they always ran (the pool starts
lazily on the first step it would take, and uses each worker from the step after it reports ready).
346 measured 144 / 166 / 284 ms under node for workers importing the evaluator; these are the same
order. The copy is paid only by a frame allocated before the host opted in (a host that opts in before
it prepares its sources pays none): the plan copies each vector the step reads into shared memory
once. The plan itself — the step and environment through the wire and back, and the result slots — is
1 to 2 ms at a million rows; Phase 375's rebuild of every mask in every worker (8.7 to 9.7 ms a million
rows under node, D13 item 5) is gone: a worker's rebuild now reads the frame's own bytes, 0.02 to 0.04
ms at any size.

The node `derive` at a million rows is 1.47 times faster (its carrier was a generic JavaScript array,
now a typed one beside its byte mask). The node `filter` reads 3.6 per cent slower at a million rows and
6 per cent at 100,000 — the sittings overlap at a million (102 to 111 before, 106 to 107 after) and
not at 100,000 (9.8 to 10.5 before, 10.4 to 10.6 after): a byte read and a comparison where a boolean
was read, a few per cent of a row that costs 100 ns. .NET moves within its noise both ways.

**The corpus harness** (`benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks/Node`, 262's: 71 cases, its batched
median of 10 samples) under node: 19 cases read 5 per cent or more faster, among them the prepared
chain at 100,000 rows (0.69 to 0.71 of before), the window (0.92), `filter-groupby-filter` at 20,000
(0.82) and `group-and-sum, float` at 100,000 (0.83); and 7 cases read 5 per cent or more slower. One
of the seven is a hand-written arm this change does not touch (`lines: hand arm` at 1,000 rows, 1.05),
which is this harness's noise floor. Four have before and after sittings that overlap: `filter-groupby:
refresh` at 1,000 rows (0.262–0.271 before, 0.271–0.281 after), `filter, decimal` (28.0–32.0 against
27.5–31.0), `group-and-sum, int` (15.5–21.0 against 14.5–19.3, while `float` reads 0.83) and `join,
float` at 100,000 (211–219 against 207–242, while `int` reads 1.03 and `decimal` 0.98). The two
cases that are NOT noise are the sheet's evaluator arms at 100,000 rows, the whole pipeline from the
`Table` boundary in to the boundary out: `lines` 37.75 → 43.50 ms (1.15) and `byRegion` 24.50 → 28.75
ms (1.17), consistently across six sittings in two rounds. Measured apart, every phase of `lines` is
faster (the boundary in 11.9 → 10.1 ms, the two derives 7.5 → 6.5 and 6.1 → 5.9, the boundary out 6.1
→ 5.8, as 3-second means); composed, mean over 8 seconds of back-to-back calls, `lines` is 33.6–33.8 ms
before and 35.4–37.7 after (+5 to +12 per cent) and `byRegion` 22.8–23.3 before and 22.1–24.0 after.
What the composed pipeline pays is collection: each call now allocates about 3.1 MB of typed-array
backing stores where it allocated 1.6 MB (the masks and the derived carriers were heap arrays), and
the engine collects for those. The same pipeline with its masks drawn from one reused buffer (an
experiment, not shipped: it retains memory) runs in 26 ms, faster than before. On .NET the corpus
harness moves within its noise in both directions (its hand-written arms move as much as its
evaluator arms), as it must: the .NET code is unchanged.

So "no sequential figure regresses" holds on .NET, and for every step-level figure under node but
the `filter`'s few per cent; it does not hold for the sheet's whole evaluator at 100,000 rows under
node, which pays 5 to 17 per cent (by method) in collection for the typed masks the pool needs.
`DECISIONS.md` D14 records the trade for the operator.
