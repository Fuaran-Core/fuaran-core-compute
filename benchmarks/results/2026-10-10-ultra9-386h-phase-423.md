# Compute benchmarks — 2026-10-10, Intel Core Ultra 9 386H, Phase 423 (the frame is a view over Core `1.0.0`'s typed columns)

Phase 423 raises the substrate to Core `1.0.0`, whose `Column` is a typed vector, and makes the
evaluator's frame a VIEW over it: `prepare` / `Frame.ofTable` borrows each column's storage and
`toTable` adopts the frame's arrays, temporal columns are carried as day counts and seconds, and the
code that converted at the boundary is gone. This file records the boundary's own cost on this tree,
the Phase 342 chain re-run (which measured exactly the cost this phase removes), and the dated
pipeline that joins the corpus with it (Phase 422's task, carried here).

**A different machine from every earlier file.** Intel Core Ultra 9 386H, 2.10 GHz, 16 cores, Windows
11 (10.0.26200), .NET SDK 10.0.300, Release, BenchmarkDotNet v0.15.8, one launch, three warm-ups, ten
measured iterations (`dotnet run -c Release --project benchmarks/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Benchmarks --
--filter '*Chain*' '*Dated*' '*Boundary*'`). No figure here is comparable to an i7-9700 or i7-8650U
figure as a TIME; the ratios within one run, and the allocations, are what this file claims.

|---|---|

The bytes are the four validity masks the frame builds for `AllValid` columns (100,000 bytes each): no value is copied. | The table out of a kept result | Below a nanosecond: a `Prepared`'s table is built once and memoised, so the benchmark reads the memo — recorded as a limitation, not a figure (see below). The boundary out is measured through the chain instead |

| The same at 1,000 and 10,000 rows | 0.82 and 0.73 of the time; 0.90 and 0.89 of the bytes |

## The boundary

`Boundary.PrepareIn` is `DataFrame.prepare orders` over the sheet's `orders` (`id:int, region:string,
qty:int, price:float`), every column `AllValid`.

| Rows | Median | Allocated |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 1.33 µs | 5,456 B |
| 10,000 | 9.85 µs | 41,456 B |
| 100,000 | 131.1 µs | 401,962 B |

The allocation is one byte per row per column — the validity masks the frame builds where Core's
column carries none (`Validity.AllValid`) — plus the frame record; the values are the column's own
arrays. A column carrying a `Mask` is borrowed outright on .NET.

**`Boundary.TableOut` measured the memo.** `DataFrame.toTable` over a kept `Prepared` answers the
table it built on the first call, so the ten measured iterations read a field (0.6–0.75 ns). The
figure is recorded so the harness is not re-run expecting more of it; the cost of building a table
out of a frame is read off the chain below, where the Table-chained arm pays it at every hop.

## The chain (Phase 342, re-run)

`Corpus.chainPipelines`, as Phase 342 defined them: `lines`, then the big lines with a net amount,
then the tax beside the net projected to four columns. Table-chained pays `prepare` and a table out
at every hop; prepared-chained pays one of each.

| Arm | Rows | Median | Ratio | Allocated | Alloc ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TableChained | 1,000 | 47.2 µs | 1.00 | 129.1 KB | 1.00 |
| PreparedChained | 1,000 | 38.3 µs | 0.82 | 116.1 KB | 0.90 |
| TableChained | 10,000 | 409.4 µs | 1.00 | 807.2 KB | 1.00 |
| PreparedChained | 10,000 | 294.8 µs | 0.73 | 715.8 KB | 0.89 |
| TableChained | 100,000 | 3,757 µs | 1.00 | 7,572 KB | 1.00 |
| PreparedChained | 100,000 | 2,830 µs | 0.74 | 6,702 KB | 0.89 |

What the ratio's move says: on 2026-10-04 the prepared chain was 0.38 of the Table chain's time and
0.48 of its bytes, because each table out boxed every cell and each `prepare` packed every column
again. With both directions views, the two extra crossings cost about a quarter of the chain, and
most of what remains is the selection the second hop's filter leaves (the kept result is gathered
dense, once, on either arm). `evalToPrepared` still pays: the same result in three quarters of the
time; it no longer pays for the boundary's conversions, because there are none.

## The dated pipeline (Phase 422's task, carried by Phase 423)

`Corpus.datedTable`: the sheet's `orders` with a `day: date` column, every order dated inside 2026,
built from day counts straight (`Column.ofDates`). `Corpus.byMonthPipeline`: a filter keeping the
second and third quarters (`day >= 2026-04-01 and day < 2026-10-01`, two date literals against the
date column), `amount = qty * price`, `month = DatePart("month", day)`, then a group by month with the
total and the count. `Corpus.handByMonth` is the hand arm over the arrays, the range test on the day
counts and the month off `TemporalText.civilOfDays`; `--check` asserts the two agree at every size
before anything is timed.

| Arm | Rows | Median | Ratio | Allocated | Alloc ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ByMonthHandArm | 1,000 | 7.06 µs | 1.00 | 16.7 KB | 1.00 |
| ByMonthEvaluator | 1,000 | 136.0 µs | 19.3 | 507.0 KB | 30.4 |
| ByMonthHandArm | 10,000 | 68.8 µs | 1.00 | 157.8 KB | 1.00 |
| ByMonthEvaluator | 10,000 | 1,211 µs | 17.7 | 4,747 KB | 30.1 |
| ByMonthHandArm | 100,000 | 710 µs | 1.00 | 1,568 KB | 1.00 |
| ByMonthEvaluator | 100,000 | 11,126 µs | 16.3 | 47,158 KB | 30.1 |

**Held bytes.** The date column is 4 bytes a row (the day count) beside a mask of 1 byte a row where
the column is not `AllValid` — 400 KB at 100,000 rows, where the canonical text held 10 characters a
row in a string per row. **Read cost.** The range filter compiles the two comparisons over the date
column's day node and `DatePart` reads the month off the day count; the ratio against the hand arm
(16–19x) is the sheet's shape (the `byRegion` node reads 8–10x on the i7-9700 files), not a temporal
cost: the group by month and the two aggregates allocate what the string-keyed group did.
