# Compute benchmarks — 2026-10-03, Intel Core i7-8650U, Phase 333 (the running windows resume from their earliest change; a declined refresh costs its evaluation)

Phase 333 admits the prefix-fold windows (`rowNumber`, `cumulSum`, `cumulMax`, `cumulMin`) to the
seam as a RESUMED run: the step records the ordering it scanned and the cell at each position, and a
refresh copies each touched partition's prior run up to its earliest changed position and continues
the reference's own scan (`DataFrame.windowColumnOver`) from there, seeded with the cell before it.
It also takes out of a DECLINED refresh the structural comparison of its pipeline against the
state's (a cell-by-cell walk of an embedded relation on every tick) and the second row count of the
source, and it refuses, by name, a `Filter` over a selection naming a row past its vectors.

## Instrument

.NET 10 (SDK 10.0.203 band), Release. Two instruments, because the machine was SHARED and loaded for
the whole session (other workers' builds and suites held every core; the calibration bracket read the
load, and wall-clock figures moved by a factor of two between back-to-back runs of one binary):

- **The clock leg** (`dotnet run --project tests/Fuaran.Core.Compute.Tests -c Release -- --clock-leg`),
  the gate's own instrument, on the phase's tree: the counted window of "the table-fed tick on every
  corpus node" (batched best of five, the ratio within one run, Phase 285's calibration bracketing
  every cell).
- **A cycle probe**: a console program compiled once against the phase's tree and once against
  42723dc (the pre-phase tree, exported whole), timing `DataFrame.evalPipeline` (full), `Delta.diff`
  (diff), `Incremental.refresh` over a fixed delta (refresh) and diff-plus-refresh over a fresh table
  object (tick), each as the best of five samples of the calling thread's CPU cycles
  (`QueryThreadCycleTime`; a sample spans at least 2 x 10^8 cycles), a forced collection before each.
  A thread's cycles do not count the time another process held its core, so a ratio of two cycle
  counts is the same work measured twice whatever the load; the collections a call provokes on its
  own thread are counted. The two trees ran alternately, two or three runs each.

The shapes are the corpus nodes (`ScalingTests`' copy), plus three window shapes the corpus does not
hold: the same `CumulSum` over five partitions, the corpus `CumulSum` table with a row APPENDED as the
edit, and `CumulMax` and `Rank` over the corpus table.

The corpus edit sets the value at row n/2 of one partition, so the resumed scan is half the table; the
five-partition shape resumes a fifth of that. What a resumed refresh still pays per row is the copy of
the run (slots and typed cells, `Array.blit`), the cell LIST the result is handed back as (n conses —
the representation's, paid by the full evaluation too), and, in place, nothing boxed for a kept row:
its cell is the prior column's. At 1,000 rows the call's fixed costs decide, as Phase 327 found, and
the per-call floor covers them. At 20,000 rows the corpus tick is at parity with the pre-phase tree in
cycles (0.98 on the clock leg), the thinnest of the three.

**What was measured on the way, and is not in the tree.** The first resumed design stored the run's
cells as a frame-long `Cell[]` and copied kept rows into intermediate arrays: the wholesale path's
refresh (which records the run) then cost 1.3 to 1.8 full evaluations, and a resumed one no less than
the wholesale one — storing freshly boxed cells into a large array is the cost Phase 324 measured on
the frame path, met again. The run's cells are therefore kept in the scan's own carrier (a float
total unboxed beside its presence, a row number as an `int[]`), the merge writes straight into the
new ordering, and the column a step reads is packed by the walk's own packer rather than unpacked
from its list and gathered. A forward walk of the prior cell list through `List.unfold` (to share
cells across an append) cost more than the boxing it saved — two allocations per element — and was
taken out; the sharing is the in-place case's `List.mapi`.

The overhead Phase 325 measured (45.9 ms against 28.4 at 100,000 rows) was the refresh comparing its
pipeline with the state's by structural equality before anything else: an F# list of transforms has no
identity shortcut, so the `Embedded` right relation of the join — 100,000 rows of cells — was walked
on every tick. Measured alone on this tree: 6.4 ms at 100,000 rows and 0.080 ms at 1,000 (against a
full evaluation of 0.27 ms), the same object or an equal copy alike. The refresh now takes the identity
first (`samePipeline`, `sameEnv`), and the reference path reads the source's row count from the
boundary it prepares rather than walking the first column again. The pivot and the distinct carry no
embedded relation, so they had little to lose and read at parity on both trees; the 1,000-row
distinct's spread (0.85 to 1.55 over six runs of both trees) is a 0.2-million-cycle call's noise.

## node

- **The probe** (a scratch project compiled by Fable, node v22.20.0): the filter over
  the malformed selection prints `type error: the frame's selection names row 7, past its 3-row
  vectors` on the kernel path and the compiled path, the .NET suite's line; the window law (one window
  of a drawn prefix fold over a drawn partitioning and direction, five delta classes, int / mixed /
  decimal values) holds over 400 draws with 0 disagreements, and 44 draws are red under the perturbed
  seed. Fable fills a fresh reference array with `null` where .NET does, and the seed's "none" is the
  same `null` on both.
- **The node harness** (`Node/run-node.ps1 -Runs 1`) compiled and ran to exit 0 on the phase's tree:
  every corpus agreement it asserts before timing held, the scaling pipelines' refresh against the
  full evaluation among them, and the decimal-vector law (8,400 pipelines over 400 seeds). Its timing
  tables are single runs on a loaded machine and are not claimed.
